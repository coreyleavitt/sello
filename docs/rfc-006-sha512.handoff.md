## Open findings / forks

**Slice 1b wipe-cost blowout (2026-08-21) -- RESOLVED same day on
Corey's direction; see resolution paragraph below.**
`private/sha512.nim` and its KAT/boundary/streaming coverage in
`test_sha512.nim` are implemented and pass the full RFC-006 slice 1b test
plan against real vectors: the empty-message KAT, the full CAVP ShortMsg
sweep (129/129) and LongMsg sweep (128/128) through the one-shot face,
all 9 padding-boundary lengths (0, 1, 111, 112, 127, 128, 129, 239, 240)
in both the one-shot and incremental-split registers (splits placed
exactly at the 0/1/111/112/127/128 thresholds), 2-arg/3-arg one-shot
agreement with the concatenated 1-arg call, init-after-finish streaming
reuse, and a whole-object wipe smoke test. `scripts/test.sh` is green in
full (12 unit files + 5 property suites) with this module compiled in.

The measure-record-revert wipe-cost step (Hygiene section's own recorded
risk point), however, measured the compression core's per-call
`ct.wipe(W)`/`ct.wipe(v)` cost at roughly **50% overhead**, not the
RFC's stated single-digit-percent expectation -- and clear past this
slice's own recorded ">25%" escalation trigger. Methodology: a throwaway
driver (session scratchpad, never committed) one-shot-hashing a fixed
128-byte input (2 `compress` calls per hash) 1,000,000 times per run,
`-d:release`, `std/times` wall-clock, compiled twice (with the core's
wipes intact, and with them stubbed out behind a temporary
`-d:selloSha512NoWipe` guard that has since been deleted from
`sha512.nim` per the RFC's own "must not survive" instruction regardless
of outcome). Two independent sample sets, 8 total timed runs, tight
non-overlapping clusters:
- With wipes: 1921-2371 ns/call (mean ~2145 ns/call across both sets).
- Without wipes: 1394-1520 ns/call (mean ~1460 ns/call across both sets).
- Ratio: roughly 1.47x, i.e. ~47-50% overhead depending on which sample
  set is used -- consistently and reproducibly far above both the RFC's
  "single-digit percent" expectation and the recorded ">25%" stop bar.

Three candidate resolutions were surfaced with the escalation (accept
the cost and amend the RFC; coarsen the wipe granularity; re-measure) --
Corey declined all three and directed a best-in-class redesign instead.

Resolution (2026-08-21, Corey's direction): attack both cost factors
rather than weakening the posture. (1) Rolling 16-word message schedule
-- W[t] depends only on W[t-2]/W[t-7]/W[t-15]/W[t-16], so the 640-byte
80-word array need never exist; secret-bearing scratch shrinks from 704
to 192 bytes (the cheapest wipe is of memory that never held the
secret). (2) A word-granular `wipe[N](var array[N, uint64])` sibling
overload in `private/ct.nim` (volatile per-uint64 stores, same barrier
and `{.noinline.}` shape; the audited byte-generic primitive untouched;
overload resolution routes word arrays there automatically), cutting the
store count another 8x. Net: 24 volatile word stores per block instead
of 704 byte stores (~30x less wipe work), per-call wipe discipline
preserved verbatim. The RFC's Hygiene section carries the same record
as an in-place dated amendment. Slice 1b's implementation resumes with
this design: refactor under the already-green KAT/boundary estate,
re-measure under the identical methodology (single-digit expectation
restored; the >25% escalation trigger stands), then gate and commit.
